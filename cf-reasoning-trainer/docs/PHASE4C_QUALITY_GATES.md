# Proposed gates before Phase 4C

Phase 4C is not approved. The current synthetic benchmark checks software behavior; it provides no evidence that a model produces correct Codeforces mathematics.

## Per-analysis gates

1. Current authorized statement, immutable source hash, eligible published rating, complete statement and approved inference scope.
2. Schema-valid independent derivation before classification; no tags/editorials/reference labels in initial input. Disclosure of recognition and unresolved claims.
3. Passing applicable samples and bounded tests, with real execution evidence; program/prose consistency and asymptotic bounds reviewed. Unsupported checks stay explicit and require a reviewed alternative protocol. The current fixture acceptance rule is stricter: unsupported execution cannot pass.
4. Two independent human assessments accept algorithm, proof, main discovery, supporting observations, learner bottleneck, prerequisites, difficulty rationale and training usefulness. Any unresolved essential disagreement blocks acceptance. A known wrong theorem or proof may never be called verified.
5. Classification proposals normalized through review; no unsupported new concept or misleading similarity is promoted. The tag-removal test must succeed.
6. No unresolved critique contradiction or failing evidence. An independent audit can suspend prior acceptance. Corrected candidates receive new immutable artifacts and fresh evidence.
7. Evidence costs and provenance are recorded; unknown paid usage is reconciled. Evaluation acceptance itself does not publish a catalog record or make it accessible to the training planner.

## Pilot decision gates

The future pilot must contain 45 distinct, permitted real problems: five with current official ratings in each band 1200 through 2000. Include several difficult multi-observation problems, contrasts and diverse mechanisms; do not replace missing bands with guessed ratings. Every accepted pilot result must have educational review, and all suspected errors must be investigated before any batch approval.

Proposed small-pilot thresholds for a **limited next evaluation**, not automatic corpus backfill:

- Zero known incorrect analyses or invalid proofs promoted; zero unresolved critical disagreements in any accepted record.
- At least 41/45 (91.1%) independently accepted algorithms, 41/45 proofs, and 41/45 primary insights in the candidate routing arm; report each separately with unresolved/unreviewed counts. At least four of five per real rating band must pass those criteria. Any failing problem remains unavailable even if a cohort threshold passes.
- At least 41/45 useful reasoning fingerprints, with no algorithm-only classification accepted. Every promoted fingerprint passes the learner-bottleneck rubric.
- Independent audit of **every initially accepted** pilot record, with zero discovered false promotions. Any false promotion halts the next batch and triggers targeted remediation/re-evaluation.
- All usage/costs reconciled; no unresolved paid calls. Choose routing from paired reviews and additional cost per accepted result. No numerical escalation-benefit threshold is assumed before actual costs and quality tradeoffs are visible.

These thresholds are deliberately only pilot decision aids. Even 45 independent successes give a roughly 92.1% lower two-sided 95% Wilson bound; zero observed failures does not establish a negligibly small failure rate. Five cases per band provide little statistical power. The current related synthetic variants are even less informative. A full-corpus autonomous verification claim would need much larger independent/adversarial validation and an approved human-review strategy; no finite pilot makes all future outputs correct.

After a satisfactory paid pilot and explicit quality review, propose only a bounded next batch with authorized sources, cost estimate, stop limit and sampling/audit plan. The user must approve Phase 4C and its paid scope separately. Budget exhaustion, an ambiguous charge, failed validation or a critical review finding stops the run; there is no automatic expansion of scope.

## Evidence-driven repair

Classify actual failures with the rubric codes. Fix missing necessity/sufficiency obligations in solving prompts; fix misleading labels or bottlenecks in the classification prompt/taxonomy; improve legal small-case generators and independent references for missed counterexamples. Test the change on held-out examples and re-review affected artifacts. Do not endlessly escalate to a stronger model or present mock-injected failures as discoveries about a real model. Preserve earlier failures and denominators when reporting improvements.
